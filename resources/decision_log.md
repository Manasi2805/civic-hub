What approach did we take, and what did we reject? (~150 words)
Our approach: Trust score derived from coordinate bounding precision, EXIF/media presence, and 100-meter duplicate clustering, routing scores between 50% and 74% to a "Needs Verification" staff backlog.Incoming submissions take GPS latitude/longitude, category type, description text, and image payloads through verification_engine.py. The system validates coordinates against Mysuru municipal boundary GeoJSON polygons, scans nearby open reports within 100 meters for duplicates, and calculates a 0–100% confidence rating. Submissions scoring $\ge 75\%$ route directly to zonal maintenance queues, while lower-scoring items are held for supervisor triage.Alternative we considered and rejected: Mandatory OTP/Aadhaar-based identity verification before permitting report lodging.This required citizens to complete two-factor authentication via SMS or DigiLocker before lodging any ticket. It appeared attractive because it virtually eliminated automated sybil spam, identity spoofing, and frivolous prank reports.
Why did we reject it? 
The trade-offWe compared both options across filing friction, spam resistance, low-connectivity behavior, and rapid delivery. Mandatory identity verification creates massive drop-off rates on mobile devices, especially in areas with poor cellular signal or during SMS gateway timeouts, and integrating third-party identity APIs carries substantial development overhead. In contrast, our session-backed heuristic allows citizens to draft and submit reports instantly with zero friction, gracefully accommodates patchy 2G or 3G networks, and fits within our lightweight FastAPI service architecture.The friction-to-submission ratio decided our choice. By choosing heuristic verification over strict identity gates, we accepted the cost that our system is vulnerable to coordinated local manipulation: an individual cycling through private browsing sessions or separate devices can file duplicate complaints that our system must catch purely by physical location radius rather than verified identity.
 What breaks at the scale of all of Mysuru?
 At citywide scale across all 65 wards and peri-urban areas, three bottlenecks emerge. First, our in-memory spatial duplicate check scales at order $O(n)$ across roughly 15,000 active complaints, introducing hundreds of milliseconds of latency per write. We would resolve this by migrating to PostgreSQL with PostGIS spatial indexing or geohash bucketing. Second, major event surges such as the Dasara festival spike daily volume to roughly 3,500 reports; our 50% to 74% review band would generate nearly 700 daily manual tickets, quickly overwhelming city staff. We would fix this by implementing perceptual image hashing to automatically merge visual duplicates. Third, saving uncompressed media directly to local disk storage will exhaust host capacity after roughly 50 GB of image uploads, which we would solve by routing image uploads directly to S3-compatible cloud object storage using pre-signed URLs.The single change we would make first is migrating our local SQLite database to PostgreSQL with PostGIS spatial indexing, ensuring all high-volume proximity queries execute in sub-millisecond logarithmic time during citywide reporting surges.
